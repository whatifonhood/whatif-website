Most people who lose money in this corner of crypto do not lose it to a clever exploit. They lose it to a copy of something real, or to a page that asked them to approve one transaction too many. These are the checks that catch nearly all of it.

## Compare the contract address character by character

A token is its contract address. The name and the ticker are not unique — anyone can deploy a token called $IF with the same logo. Only the address is the thing itself.

Copy the address from a source you already trust, and compare it to the one on whatever page you are about to buy from. Not the first four characters and the last four — those are trivially matched by anyone generating vanity addresses. **The whole string, or nothing.**

If a site shows you a shortened address with an ellipsis in the middle and no way to see the rest, that is not a design decision. Leave.

## Nobody legitimate needs your seed phrase

Not support. Not a giveaway. Not a "wallet validation" step. Not a migration. There is no situation in which a real project needs the twelve or twenty-four words that control your wallet, because those words _are_ your wallet.

Anyone asking for them is stealing from you, and no amount of official-looking branding changes that.

## Read what you are approving

A swap needs permission to spend one token, in one amount. If a page asks you to approve unlimited spending of something you did not intend to trade, or asks for a signature whose content you cannot read, stop.

Wallet approval screens are ugly and easy to click past. That is precisely what the people writing malicious contracts are relying on.

## Check the liquidity, not the chart

A chart can look wonderful on almost no money. What matters is how much liquidity sits in the pool, because that is what you would be selling into. A token with a beautiful chart and four thousand dollars of liquidity cannot give you your money back.

## Assume the reply is fake

Reply guys under an announcement post, a "support" account that messages you first, a second Telegram group with a slightly different handle — these are the most reliable way people get caught. Real projects publish their channels in one place; go to that place yourself rather than following a link somebody handed you.
